## Enrol a cluster

<Steps>
<Step>

### Get a trust token

On the cluster, as an Incus administrator:

```bash
incus config trust add incuscloud --quiet
```

The token holds the cluster's addresses and certificate fingerprint, and works once.

</Step>
<Step>

### Enrol it

**Clusters** › **Enrol cluster**:

- **Name**: lowercase. Cluster `prod` is reached at `https://prod.clusters.example.com`.
- **Trust token**: the token.
- **Labels**: optional, `key=value` per line. Automatic placement can pick clusters by label, for
  example `region=eu`.

Through the API: `POST /api/v1/clusters` with `{"name": "prod", "token": "…"}`.

</Step>
<Step>

### Wait for it to converge

IncusCloud connects on the token's addresses, checks the certificate against the token, adds its
own client certificate, and then configures the cluster:

- `oidc.issuer`, `oidc.client.id`, `oidc.audience` and `oidc.claim`;
- `authorization.openfga.*`, with the cluster's own store.

It then reads the inventory. The cluster shows as ready within one reconcile interval (60 s by
default).

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout title="With the internal CA">
  Every member of the cluster must trust `ca.crt` before anyone signs in to it. See
  [Certificates](/docs/install/certificates#b-the-internal-ca).
</Callout>

## A cluster's settings

IncusCloud's own settings for a cluster are on its **Configure** tab › **IncusCloud**:

- **General**: its status, address, certificate fingerprint, labels, and when it was last seen and reconciled.
- **Command line**: the `incus remote add` command for it.
- **Administration**: **Reconcile now**, **Edit labels** and **Delete**.
- **Tenants**: the tenants placed on it, with **Place** and **Remove**.
- **Balancing**: its [balancing policy](/docs/guides/balancing).

## Place a tenant

On the cluster's **Tenants** section, **Place** a tenant. The reconciler creates its restricted
project and grants, and splits the tenant's quota again. See
[Tenants and placements](/docs/concepts/tenants).

**Remove** takes the tenant's grants off the cluster but keeps its project, which may still hold
instances. A tenant with Kubernetes clusters there can't be removed.

## When something is wrong

A cluster whose reconcile fails shows as degraded, with the reason. A cluster whose inventory fails
keeps its last inventory and shows the error. Common causes are in
[Troubleshooting](/docs/operations/troubleshooting).

## Delete a cluster

Remove the tenants placed on it first, then **Delete**. IncusCloud then:

1. unsets its OIDC and OpenFGA settings on the cluster, so it stops trusting IncusCloud's tokens and
   grants;
2. removes its own certificate.

Tenants' projects and instances stay on the cluster. A cluster with tenant Kubernetes clusters
can't be deleted, even with `force`.
